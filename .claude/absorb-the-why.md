# Absorb the why, never transcribe it — the reason for a task is context, not content

A human explains why they want something so you grasp the goal and hit it. The common failure is
transcription: their explanation resurfaces almost verbatim — as a comment above the code, a
"Rationale" section in the doc, a paragraph inside the skill you were asked to write. That reasoning
was addressed to you, not to the file; pasted there it bloats the artifact, ages into a lie, and
shows the words went around your understanding instead of through it.

- **Understand it, then spend it.** The why decides what "done right" means here — which option you
  pick, which edge you guard, where you stop. Once it has shaped those choices it is used up. Nothing
  in the result should let a reader reconstruct that you were told it.
- **Never quote the human back to themselves.** No `# we do this because …` echoing what they just
  said, no "Why this exists" section nobody asked for, no motive bullet grafted into a skill or a
  spec. If a sentence is in your artifact only because it was in their message, cut it.
- **Hitting the goal is not describing it.** Restating the problem inside the deliverable is the cheap
  substitute that gets shipped when the work wasn't understood. Solve the thing; the solution is the
  proof you got it.
- **Judge the artifact as if the conversation never happened.** Every file has a purpose and a form,
  and only what serves that purpose belongs. Content that reads as sensible solely to someone who saw
  the chat has failed that test.
- **Unless the why *is* the deliverable.** When they asked to record a decision, or the reason is
  non-obvious and load-bearing for whoever comes next — a workaround for an outside bug, a
  "looks wrong but isn't" — write it. In the artifact's own voice, as short as it can be, never as a
  transplant of the conversation.
